Staying Grounded

Practical reminders to keep nearby. Work through the course to explore the situations and explanations.

Back to course

Need support now? Pause the course and use your school’s approved support or emergency routes.

Pause and choose

  • Notice one observable reaction. Look around or focus on an ordinary object if comfortable. Choose one safe, useful next action. Stop any exercise that does not suit you.

Protect your attention

  • Agree when work information needs checking. Mute non-essential commentary. A boundary does not close legitimate complaint, safeguarding or protected-disclosure routes.

Useful words

  • To a parent: “I hear your concern. I cannot discuss individual cases here. I can explain the appropriate route.”
  • To a friend: “Please don’t forward general commentary to me.”
  • To a colleague: “Would listening, practical help or some space be most useful?”
  • For a boundary: “I can help with this part; I need support with the rest.”

Know when to escalate

  • For a safeguarding concern, listen without investigating, avoid promising secrecy and follow the applicable procedure promptly. Use an alternative or independent route if the usual contact is implicated.
  • For abuse or harassment, seek appropriate support and report through the relevant work/platform route. Preserve only safe, permitted evidence securely; do not circulate it. Do not copy or redistribute suspected illegal material or sexual imagery involving children.
  • For a direct or immediate safety threat, seek immediate local help.

Support and perspective

  • Use the support arrangements shared by your school. If the Head or usual contact is involved in the concern, seek the appropriate alternative, independent or external route.
  • Persistent, worsening or disruptive distress is a reason to consider qualified support. This guide is educational, not clinical care.
  • Education Support resources use UK terminology; their direct services have UK eligibility conditions. Check eligibility and local alternatives.
  • Nothing here requires positivity, personal disclosure or public defence of the school. Organisational problems need organisational action.
